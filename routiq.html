Published research

Consistency is more than a counter.

Lally and colleagues studied contextual repetition over 12 weeks with 96 volunteers. Automaticity trajectories varied; the abstract reports that one missed opportunity did not materially affect formation.

Question for Routiq: can progress feedback explain recorded activity without promising a universal habit-formation deadline or treating one miss as total loss?

Lally et al. · publisher abstract
Abstract-level review; self-report/modeling, narrower model-fit coverage. This does not settle streak calculations or repeated-miss behavior.

Published research

Reminders carry tradeoffs.

A 30-volunteer notification study reported less distraction and greater productivity under suppression, alongside anxiety about responsiveness for some participants. Others did not share that anxiety.

Question for Routiq: what reminder timing and control help in the actual routine context? Neither more alerts nor blanket suppression is established as the right policy.

Pielot & Rello · author paper
Short, dated, self-reported, white-collar sample; all-device notifications, not a habit-reminder trial.

Product documentation

Meaning matters more than novelty.

Productive documents skip-inclusive totals, Loop describes a graded habit score, and Habitify already documents habit-aware AI. Similar labels can represent different rules.

Question for Routiq: are progress explanations and optional habit experiments understandable and useful compared with existing alternatives?

Documentation establishes stated capabilities; it does not measure comprehension, effectiveness or market-wide uniqueness. See the comparison below.

The concept took shape around daily decisions.

I organized the requirements around manageable daily activity, understandable progress and a useful next step. These remained provisional problems, and that framing guided the structural exploration.

Provisional hypothesis

Keep daily intentions manageable.

A person may struggle to keep intended recurring activities and recorded completion manageable through the day.

Design hypothesis: connect habit configuration and daily recording. The current build explores this through a small habit editor and Today checklist.

Validate real setup/logging effort and abandonment reasons.
Provisional hypothesis

Understand progress after interruptions.

A person may struggle to interpret what recorded history means, especially after missed or skipped opportunities.

Design hypothesis: use transparent metrics. The build shows completion counts and recorded activity; streak calculations are outside the release.

Agree metric rules, then assess actual interpretations and errors.
Provisional hypothesis

Judge a useful next step.

A person may lack enough understandable context to decide which routine adjustment is relevant and worth trying.

Design hypothesis: offer contextual guidance as optional experiments. The assignment implements prepared FAQ replies and local Today summaries.

Investigate usefulness, permitted context and overtrust; the deployed FAQ is not personalized live AI.

Make the next question part of the conversation

The first Coach release always showed the same four sample questions. I refined that interaction so the next choices relate to what the user has just asked, whether they click a suggestion or type their own question.

The backend now returns four curated follow-ups with each reply. The frontend uses those candidates, keeps the latest topic for short follow-ups and prefers questions that have not recently appeared. The question just asked is excluded.

  • Coach begins with four starter questions.
  • Each successful reply replaces them with four related choices.
  • Switching from reading to workout ideas or Today changes the suggested direction.
  • A failed request keeps the previous choices and typed question available for retry.
  • New chat restores the original four starters.

I checked all FAQ inputs, the four-question behavior, topic changes, reset and error handling. The backend tests and all frontend builds passed. I then pushed the update to GitHub and confirmed the matching Render and Vercel deployments were live.

The suggestion picker uses the existing prepared FAQ content. It adds no model calls or saved conversation history. I later checked the public app with keyboard-driven questions: each prepared reply replaced the four suggestions with four related choices. That check also revealed a focus issue when the activated suggestion disappeared, which remains open for correction and retesting.

Result: Coach now offers a relevant next step instead of repeating a fixed set of sample questions.
